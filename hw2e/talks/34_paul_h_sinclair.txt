Paul H. Sinclair (Of the Seventy)
Quick to Help, Slow to Judge
“And a little child shall lead them.” Some years ago, this scripture truly came alive to me. It happened during a ward Primary program.
That Sabbath morning, my young friend James was wearing a new suit. As the program began, James was examining some of the more interesting aspects of his new suit. What most captured his attention were two features of his jacket. On his jacket sleeves there were buttons but no holes. And on the left lapel of his jacket there was a buttonhole but no buttons. Neither the buttons nor the buttonhole seemed to be serving any real purpose.
Well, James cleverly devised a purpose for both. I anxiously watched as James delightedly buttoned his right sleeve to his left jacket lapel. Being pleased with himself quickly gave way to being panicked, because once James had buttoned his arm to his chest, even with the flexibility of a Primary child, he could not get himself loose. James had unintentionally, but quite successfully, created a one-arm straitjacket.
As James became more and more stuck, I became more and more aggravated. Baffling buttons had been put to an uncomfortable and, to me, irritating purpose. I was shaking my head in disappointment as James was about to go to the pulpit.
Just then I noticed the little girl sitting next to James. She had also witnessed James’s predicament and his growing concern. But instead of being aggravated, she wanted to help. Quickly discerning what action was needed, she reached up with her tiny, nonjudgmental fingers and popped the button out of the buttonhole. James was rescued, and the Primary program was saved!
I felt relieved but also humbled. I recognized the quiet rebuke of a misplaced button and tiny fingers reminding me that I should be trying to be more like Jesus. That small but majestic moment helped me embrace what Elder Patrick Kearon has described as “a radical reorientation” of my soul. I don’t recall the topic of the Primary program that day, but the message I received through the Holy Ghost prompted a spiritual realignment. A little child led me to learn this: that a true disciple of Jesus Christ is quick to help and slow to judge.
The Savior taught this loving principle of discipleship from the very beginning of His mortal ministry. In the Gospel of John, He ministers to a Samaritan woman. We do not know her name. We have come to know her as “the woman at the well.” Her interaction with the Lord Jesus Christ teaches many lessons. One critical lesson is the example of the Savior being quick to offer grace and transformative help.
As the conversation opened, Jesus was weary and unexpectedly asked the woman for a drink. We can only imagine how many days this woman had been coming to this well at this hour, alone—until this day. To her, it was an ordinary day—until Jesus showed up. In her ordinary days, she may have endured many judging eyes but few helping hands—until this day.
This woman may have done more to warrant judgment than merely buttoning her sleeve to her lapel. Apparently, she had a series of broken relationships and was, at that moment, living with a man who was not her husband. But what do we actually know about her circumstances? Was she widowed? divorced? abandoned? We do not know. Now, please don’t misunderstand me. Her story is very important, but these are not the judgmental details we need to know about her journey.
What did Christ focus on instead? Help. The offer of “living water.” The Great Redeemer of all knew absolutely everything about this unnamed woman, and He came to her to be a living witness of living water. He offered her grace and transformative help.
He spoke to the woman with dignity, not shame. He earnestly listened to her questions and responded with teaching, not ridicule. He did not hold her hostage to her history. He trusted her with truth. Jesus Christ reminds us that to help people transform their lives, we must look not only to their past—we must look to their possible. She walked to the well that day as a pariah. She walked away from the well with a purpose. That is precisely what “quick to help and slow to judge” looks like.
Every day, missionaries meet people at modern-day wells all around the world. They declare that Jesus is the Christ, the great “I Am.” They find, teach, baptize, and confirm people who may be in fear of judgment but in need of help. As disciples of Jesus Christ, we are invited to reorient our souls so we may help friends and new members in more Christlike ways. President Dallin H. Oaks has plainly and directly invited us to “seek to be holy, like our Savior.” Being quick to help and slow to judge is a simple and deeply encouraging way for each of us to be more holy, to be more like our Savior, to be His true disciples.
I pray that when these modern-day men and women at the well meet you and me, they will know they have “come home.” I pray they will recognize in you and me striving disciples of Jesus Christ, ready to fill them with living and loving water. I testify that when we help people, one by one, in our own small and simple ways, rather than judge them, we are preparing the world for the Second Coming of Jesus Christ. I so witness and testify in the name of Jesus Christ, amen.
